MatchMaker · Stage 1 · Decision Record

The registry doesn't say who ran the trial.

So we stopped trying to make it say that — and built something the data can actually support.

What we set out to do

MatchMaker helps our sales team find sponsors who are about to outsource a clinical trial, before the RFP goes out. Step one was teaching a model to recognise the difference between a trial a sponsor runs in-house and one they hand to a CRO.

To learn that difference, a model needs examples — trials where we already know the answer. The obvious place to look was ClinicalTrials.gov, the public registry where every industry trial is recorded. We pulled 2,000 of them.

What we found instead

Roughly three in four clinical trials are outsourced to a CRO. Our registry sample identifies one in seventy. The registry records who paid for a trial, not who ran it. Measured on 2,000 industry trials · ClinicalTrials.gov API v2

The two approaches

Plan A · abandoned

Learn from labelled examples

Treat every trial naming a CRO as a confirmed outsourcing case, every trial without one as in-house, and let the model learn the pattern between them.

This is the textbook approach, and it's what we assumed we'd do.

It needs roughly 30 clean examples of each outcome to produce anything trustworthy. We had 28 on one side — and no way to know whether the other 1,972 were genuinely in-house or simply undocumented.

Blocked — the examples don't exist, and the ones that do aren't a fair sample
Plan B · adopted

Score against known drivers

Skip the guesswork. Build a transparent score from the five things the industry already knows drive outsourcing: number of trial sites, enrolment size, how much clinical infrastructure the sponsor has, trial phase, and therapeutic complexity.

Then check that score against the 28 trials we can confirm — using them to test the score rather than to train it.

Nothing is invented, every input is defensible, and a rep can see exactly why an account ranked where it did.

Adopted — honest, explainable, and testable against real cases

The strongest evidence isn't a statistic — it's who the score puts at the top. Loxo Oncology, BeiGene, MacroGenics, Astex, Adamas: small and mid-size oncology biotechs running Phase 3 trials across 70 to 190 sites. These are companies with deep science and no global clinical operations arm. They are exactly the profile that has to outsource, and the score found them without being told what to look for.

Is it just picking Phase 3 trials

Fig. 2 — Average outsourcing score by trial phase

Phase clearly matters, and it should: late-stage trials are bigger, longer and more expensive to run, so they get outsourced more often. The curve rises through Phase 3 and drops at Phase 4, which is what anyone in the industry would predict.

But phase alone doesn't decide the ranking. Phase counts for 15% of the score. Of our 392 Phase 3 trials, only 116 reached the top-scoring 10% — meaning seven in ten Phase 3 trials did not make the list. Site count and sponsor infrastructure are what separate them.

What we will and won't claim

The claimWhere it stands
The registry rarely identifies the CRO Measured directly: 1.4% of 2,000 trials. This is our finding, not a citation.
Our score ranks CRO-run trials higher Directionally yes — 1.79× lift in the top decile, and the separation runs the right way.
That result is statistically proven No. With 28 confirmable cases the margin of error is wide. We report it as directional and say so.
The 28 are a fair sample of CRO-run trials No. Trials that name their CRO are unusual by definition. Useful as a sanity check, not as proof.
Public data can identify who wins the deal No — and that's the point. Only our own win/loss history can. That's Stage 2, and it's the part no competitor can copy.

Why this makes the case stronger

We set out to prove a model could read the registry and tell us who outsources. What we proved is that the registry can't tell anyone that — and we have the number to back it up.

Citeline, GlobalData and Salesmotion all mine the same public sources we do. They hit the same 1.4% ceiling. Public data can tell you a trial is coming and roughly how likely it is to be outsourced. It cannot tell you who will win the work.

That answer sits in Medidata's Salesforce win/loss history — who we bid against, who won, and why we lost. Stage 1 finds the accounts worth calling. Stage 2 is where the advantage lives.
